## 1. Problem

What a run cost is known only per launch, inside the sealed receipts, in each client's own words: Claude Code's result carries a USD figure and per-model token counts, of which the return receipt keeps `total_cost_usd`, the model names and `num_turns`; Copilot's result carries `premiumRequests` and durations (kept as `usage`) and its session state a cumulative token count (not kept); Codex's `turn.completed` event carries token counts that are not kept at all. A judgment's return receipt keeps no client facts. Nothing sums any of it per story, per epic, per role or per model: the cost table in [getting started](getting-started.md#costs) and the launch table in [the recorded run](example-run.md) were built by a script reading the receipts by hand. Time is scattered the same way (launch and return times per run, turns per drive summary). And no bound exists on money: a run that spends is stopped by turns, attempts and the deadline, never by what it has cost.

## 2. Goal and non-goals

**Goal.** One readable bill per story and per epic, computed from the receipts, with the coordinator's own observations (launches, wall clock, driver turns) kept apart from the clients' claims (tokens, USD, premium requests); the same numbers in the summaries, in `epic status` and in the spec's `## Coordinator Result`; and an optional money bound that stops a run the way the attempt budgets do.

**Non-goals.** A price list of our own (the coordinator never turns tokens into money: a price is a claim that ages, and the operator's subscription is billed differently from the client's list price anyway); aggregation across repositories or across epics; any change to what a worker may do; a store table (the bill is derived from evidence on read, never stored).

## 3. Decisions

### What is counted (B1–B2)

**B1. The unit is the launch, as recorded.** A row of the bill is one client launch: a worker or reviewer run (`runs/<run>/launch.json` and `return.json`) or a judgment (`judgments/<key>/receipts/launch.json` and `return.json`). From the launch record: `observed_at`, `client`, `model`, `requested_mode` (read-only is the reviewer), the assignment and its binding (its profile, role, intent and `continues`, so a row says spec author, implementation, review, a correction or a continuation); from the return: `ended_at`, `result` or the client stop, and `client_facts`. The row's *observed* part is the coordinator's: the wall clock from `observed_at` to `ended_at`, the role, the step, the outcome. Its *reported* part is the client's, copied from `client_facts` and never derived: tokens, USD, premium requests, the client's turn count. A launch without a return (an attempt `unknown` or still running) is a row with its observed part and `reported: null`, and counts as *unreported*. Nothing else is a source: the bill never re-parses a retained stream; the adapter reads the stream once, at return, and seals what it found (B2).

**B2. Client facts gain the client's own usage, per adapter, additively.** Nothing already recorded changes; new keys are added to `client_facts` at return:

- *Claude Code*: `usage` (the result's `input_tokens`, `cache_creation_input_tokens`, `cache_read_input_tokens`, `output_tokens`), `model_usage` (per model name: `inputTokens`, `outputTokens`, `cacheReadInputTokens`, `cacheCreationInputTokens`, `costUSD`, `costBasis`, copied as the result gives them), `duration_ms` and `duration_api_ms`, beside `total_cost_usd`, `models` and `num_turns` that are there today.
- *Codex CLI*: `usage` from the single `turn.completed` event the terminal check already validates (`input_tokens`, `cached_input_tokens`, `cache_write_input_tokens`, `output_tokens`, `reasoning_output_tokens`). Codex reports no money.
- *Copilot CLI*: `usage` stays as recorded (`premiumRequests`, `totalApiDurationMs`, `sessionDurationMs`, `codeChanges`); `session_usage` is added from the one `session.shutdown` record of the launch's own session state (`inputTokens`, `outputTokens`, `cacheReadTokens`, `cacheWriteTokens`, `reasoningTokens`, plus `requests`) when that record exists; absent otherwise.
- *Judgments*: the judgment's `return.json` records `client_facts` the same way (today it records none), so a judgment launch is a row like any other.

The bill normalizes the token keys to one vocabulary for sums (`input`, `cached_input`, `cache_write`, `output`, `reasoning`; a client without a key contributes nothing to it) and keeps the client's original object beside the row untouched. Money appears under `usd` only where the client gave a USD figure (Claude's `total_cost_usd`); Copilot's `premium_requests` is its own column; Codex has neither. A receipt written before this decision shows what its facts hold (Claude: `usd` and the client's turns; Codex: nothing) and the missing keys read `null`.

### The bill (B3–B4)

**B3. `bill`, a read-only command without a token.** `bill --epic ID` (every story of the epic, in order, with the epic's total), `bill --feature ID` (one story or feature), `bill --lease ID` (one lease). Output, one JSON object: `{"kind": "bill", "format": 1, "scope": {...}, "launches": [row, ...], "by_story": [...], "by_role": [...], "by_client_model": [...], "totals": {...}}`. A row: `{"started_at", "ended_at", "story", "feature_id", "lease_id", "role", "step_id", "client", "model", "outcome", "observed": {"wall_seconds"}, "reported": {"client_turns", "tokens": {...}, "usd", "premium_requests", "client_facts": {...}} | null}`. Every aggregate has the same two halves: `observed: {launches, wall_seconds, driver_turns, runs}` (driver turns and runs from the lease's drive summaries) and `reported: {usd, premium_requests, tokens: {...}}` summed over the rows that report each key, with `unreported: {launches, usd, premium_requests, tokens}` giving how many rows contributed nothing to each sum. A sum is never printed without its `unreported` count. The story key comes from the feature's branch (`story/<key>`) and the mapping from assignment and request ids to features comes from the store, read-only; the receipts are read as `detail` reads them, so a sealed file that changed reads as `unknown` and its row says so.

**B4. The numbers surface without asking.** The drive summary gains `bill` for the run and for the lease so far (`{observed, reported, unreported}` as in B3, no rows); the epic summary gains `bill` for the epic so far, and `epic status` shows it per story and in total; the spec's `## Coordinator Result` (D18) gains one line, `- bill: <n> launches, <s> s client wall clock; reported by the clients: USD <x> (<k> launches without a figure), <p> premium requests, <t> tokens`, with every absent quantity left out rather than printed as zero. The wording keeps the claim marked: *reported by the clients*.

### The money bound (B5–B7)

**B5. `budgets.max_cost_usd` on the pipeline, per story.** A number from 0 to 10000 with up to two decimals; 0 or absent means no bound. Before every launch the driver sums the story's reported `usd` over every row of the feature (every lease, every revision, every chain: money is spent once, so the bound is per story, not per revision like the attempt budgets); when the sum is at or above the bound the step is `needs-human` with the reason `cost-budget` and the subjects `{used, max, unreported_launches, role}`. The stop is terminal for a plain resume, like `update-budget`: inside an epic, `epic resume --skip KEY` leaves the story, `epic resume --restart KEY --budgets FILE` starts a new chain under a wider box, where the file is `{"max_cost_usd": N}` and is recorded as the restart decision's subject beside `--roles` (E15); a story's own box in the epic input, `stories.<key>.budgets.max_cost_usd`, overrides the template the way `stories.<key>.roles` does (E14). The check counts reported money only. It never reads an unreported launch as zero in silence: the subjects and the bill carry `unreported_launches`, and a pipeline whose `max_cost_usd` is set while no role's client reports money (today: no `claude-code` role) is refused at bind (`INVALID_INPUT`, "max_cost_usd needs a role whose client reports cost"), because the bound could never fire.

**B6. `budgets.max_cost_usd` on the epic input, for the epic.** The same number, bounding the sum over the epic's stories (every story launch of the epic's features). Checked at the same point as the story bound, before every launch; when reached, the story's step stops `cost-budget` with `scope: "epic"` in the subjects and the epic summary's `epic_action` says how to go on: `epic resume --max-cost-usd N` raises the epic's bound (recorded as an intervention, `{type: "epic-budget-raised", from, to}`, in the epic's anchored state), `epic resume --skip KEY` leaves the story and the loop continues under the same bound (and stops again at the next launch if the bound is still reached). The same refusal as B5 when no role could report.

**B7. No pricing of our own.** The coordinator never multiplies tokens by a price, in the bill, in the bound or in a document. Claude's figure is the client's (its `costBasis` is recorded beside it); Copilot's unit is the premium request; Codex has tokens and no money. The [model selection guide](model-selection-guide.md) keeps quoting observed figures as before.

### What does not change (B8)

**B8.** The receipts' meaning, the sealing rules and the attempt budgets are untouched; the bill is derived on read and never written to the store; no new table, no migration; a repository driven before this decision bills what its receipts hold.

## 7. Residual uncertainty

1. Every money figure is a claim: Claude's `total_cost_usd` is computed by the client at list price (`costBasis: "list"`) and is not what a subscription invoices; the bound therefore bounds a notional figure, which is still the best available signal of runaway spend.
2. Copilot's `session.shutdown` usage is cumulative for the session and may be absent when the session ends abnormally; the row then has `premium_requests` and no tokens.
3. Codex's token counts are per thread turn; a launch is one turn by design (one attempt per process), so the single `turn.completed` is the whole launch.
4. Wall clock per launch is the coordinator's, from the launch record to the return; the client's own `duration_ms` is recorded beside it and may differ by the process start.
5. The bound is checked before a launch, so one launch may exceed it; a per-launch ceiling is not provided (the role's `timeout` and `max_turns` are the per-launch bounds).
